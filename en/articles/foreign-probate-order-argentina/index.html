Can a Foreign Probate Order Be Used in Argentina?

A probate order issued abroad can be highly relevant, but its Argentine effect depends on the order, the assets, any treaty and the precise legal result sought.

Dr. Cárdenas Enzo Javier
T° 153 F° 712 CPACF

Short answer

A foreign probate order can be used in Argentina only after the appropriate legal route is identified. It may require recognition, may serve as evidence in another Argentine proceeding, or may not be enough where Argentine real estate requires a local succession and registration basis. There is no accurate one-size-fits-all rule that every foreign probate must be exequatured or that recognition alone transfers all Argentine assets.

Begin with the effect required in Argentina

The question is not simply whether the order is “valid abroad.” Determine whether it will be used to prove heirship, access an account, register title, administer or sell property, defend a claim, or invoke res judicata. Recognition, enforcement, incidental reliance and local succession are different procedural tools.

Treaties and the competent jurisdiction come first

An applicable bilateral, Inter-American or regional treaty takes priority. If no treaty governs and the matter is before the National Civil Courts in Buenos Aires, Code of Civil and Commercial Procedure Articles 517–519 provide the general framework. Provinces have their own procedural codes, so those national provisions should not be presented as an identical rule for every Argentine court.

What a court usually verifies when domestic rules govern

The review may include finality or res judicata in the State of origin, internationally acceptable jurisdiction, proper service and opportunity to defend, authenticity, compatibility with Argentine public policy and absence of an incompatible Argentine judgment. Article 518 also requires the relevant judgment and proof of finality to be legalised and translated when the national code applies. Treaty requirements may differ.

Recognition is not a new trial on the merits

The Argentine court generally examines whether the foreign decision can have the requested effect, not whether it would have decided the inheritance in the same way. However, the court may need to apply Argentine conflict rules, exclusive local rules and public policy. A foreign decision cannot be assumed to override Argentine jurisdiction and law governing local real estate.

Argentine real estate requires special attention

Articles 2643 and 2644 provide Argentine jurisdiction over local immovable property and apply Argentine law to it. A foreign order may help prove who was recognised abroad, but the local succession and registry route must still be established. This is often the decisive issue for an heir who has completed probate overseas but cannot transfer an Argentine property.

Review the essential documents before translating the whole file

The useful first set normally includes the complete order, proof it is final or enforceable, the initiating pleading where relevant, proof of service, civil-status records, the will and a description of the Argentine asset. A legal review can then identify which originals, Apostilles or legalisations and certified Spanish translations are actually necessary.

Coordinate the foreign and Argentine proceedings

Foreign counsel can explain the effect and procedural history of the order under the law of origin; Argentine counsel determines the effect available in Argentina. Dividing those roles avoids the inaccurate assumption that one lawyer can certify foreign law without support or that a foreign order controls property everywhere.

Do you already have a probate order from another country?

Send the complete order, country and court of origin, proof of finality if available, the deceased’s last domicile and the Argentine asset involved. We can assess whether recognition, local succession or another step is the proportionate route.

Legal framework considered
  • Applicable treaty, if any, before domestic procedural law.
