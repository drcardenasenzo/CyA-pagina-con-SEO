Argentine Real Estate in an International Succession

Argentine real estate creates a strong local connection: Argentine courts may exercise jurisdiction over that property and Argentine law governs its succession.

Dr. Cárdenas Enzo Javier
T° 153 F° 712 CPACF

Short answer

When a deceased person owned real estate in Argentina, Argentine courts have jurisdiction regarding that property and Argentine succession law applies to it, even if the deceased’s last domicile and the main probate proceeding were abroad. The correct local route still depends on title, marital-property issues, the foreign record, the heirs and whether the goal is registration, partition or sale.

The Argentine rule is specific to local immovable property

Civil and Commercial Code Articles 2643 and 2644 give Argentine courts jurisdiction over real estate situated in the country and apply Argentine law to that property. The rule is asset-specific. It should not be overstated as automatic Argentine jurisdiction over every movable asset or every dispute in a worldwide estate.

Confirm title before deciding who inherited what

A registry report and the acquisition deed can reveal the registered owner, ownership share, mortgages, seizures or other restrictions. The property may also be affected by a marital-property regime. Succession rights should not be calculated from family assumptions or occupation of the property without first identifying the deceased’s actual registered and patrimonial interest.

A foreign declaration of heirs may not create registry effects by itself

A foreign probate order may be important evidence, but the Argentine registry generally needs an Argentine judicial basis capable of producing the requested registration. Depending on the case, that may involve a local succession proceeding, recognition of a foreign decision, or coordinated steps. The appropriate route should be selected after reviewing the order’s scope, finality, jurisdiction and the local title.

Registration, partition and sale are separate objectives

Recognising heirs does not necessarily divide the asset or authorise a sale. A later transaction may require agreement of all relevant parties, judicial approval in special cases, updated title reports, tax and fee compliance, and an instrument suitable for registration. The file should be planned backwards from the result the heirs want.

Heirs abroad can usually organise representation

A foreign heir may often act through a power of attorney, but the authorised acts must be precise. A power sufficient to appear in court may not authorise partition, settlement, sale, price receipt or banking steps. Authentication, Apostille or legalisation and Spanish translation depend on the place of execution and the intended use.

Occupation, expenses and income should be documented

If one heir or a third party occupies the property, or if the property produces rent, keep records of possession, taxes, building charges, repairs, leases and payments. These facts can affect administration, reimbursement claims, negotiations and the timing of a sale, even though they do not by themselves determine heirship.

Conflicts may require more than the succession declaration

Disagreement about value, use, administration, expenses or sale may require negotiation, appointment of an administrator, accounting or partition proceedings. Distance makes clear instructions and written settlement terms especially important; it does not reduce a foreign heir’s rights or eliminate procedural obligations in Argentina.

Is Argentine property blocking completion of a foreign estate?

Send the property location and title details, the deceased’s last domicile, the foreign probate order if one exists and the intended result. We can identify the Argentine stage without assuming that the entire foreign succession must be repeated.

Legal framework considered
  • Argentine Civil and Commercial Code, arts. 2335–2338, 2363 and following, 2625, 2643 and 2644.
  • The property, tax, procedural and registration rules applicable where the Argentine real estate is situated.
Official sources
  • Argentine Civil and Commercial Code
  • Argentine Code of Civil and Commercial Procedure
  • Argentine law approving the Apostille Convention
