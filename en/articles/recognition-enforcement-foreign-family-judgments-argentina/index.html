Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Family Judgments in Argentina

A judgment issued abroad may need an Argentine legal step before it can affect records, obligations, persons or assets in the country.

Dr. Cárdenas Enzo Javier
T° 153 F° 712 CPACF

Short answer

A foreign family judgment is not automatically enforceable in every Argentine case. The first step is to identify the exact effect sought and any treaty in force with the country of origin. If no treaty applies, the procedural rules of the competent Argentine jurisdiction govern. In proceedings before the National Civil Courts in Buenos Aires, Articles 517–519 set the general conditions for recognition and exequatur.

Recognition, enforcement and registration are not the same

Recognition allows the foreign decision to be accepted for a legal effect or relied on in Argentina. Enforcement seeks compulsory compliance. Registration records a recognised status or right with the competent authority. A case may require one, two or all three stages, and the application should not request a broader effect than the foreign judgment actually grants.

A treaty can change the applicable route

Argentine law gives priority to treaties in force with the State of origin and for the subject matter. The 1979 Inter-American Convention on the Extraterritorial Validity of Foreign Judgments is one example between its States Parties. Family-specific conventions may create different rules. Country, date, subject and treaty status must therefore be verified before using the domestic exequatur checklist.

The domestic requirements are jurisdiction-specific

Under National Code Article 517, the judgment must have res judicata effect in the State of origin, come from a court competent under Argentine international-jurisdiction rules, arise from the type of personal or qualifying movable-property action described by that provision, follow proper personal notice and defence, meet authenticity requirements, respect Argentine international public policy and not conflict with an earlier or simultaneous Argentine judgment. Provincial codes may formulate their requirements differently.

The merits are not ordinarily retried

Exequatur is not an appeal from the foreign judgment. The Argentine court controls the prerequisites for local effect, not whether it agrees with every factual or legal conclusion. That distinction protects final decisions while preserving due process, Argentine international jurisdiction and public policy. A new merits dispute may arise only through a legally available separate proceeding.

Finality must be examined in context

A final divorce or money judgment is different from a provisional, modifiable or emergency family order. The applicable convention may expressly recognise certain child-protection or support measures even though domestic exequatur language refers to finality. It is therefore unsafe to reject or accept a family order solely because it can later be modified; the subject-specific instrument must be checked.

Prepare the record required for the chosen route

The initial review should include the complete judgment, proof of service and participation, proof of finality or enforceability, the initiating documents if jurisdiction is not evident, and the Argentine result sought. Under National Code Articles 123 and 518, foreign-language court documents require translation by a registered public translator and the judgment must meet applicable authentication requirements. Treaties may simplify or alter formalities.

Local counsel should define the competent court and next stage

The appropriate court depends on the subject, the person or asset affected and the applicable procedural system. If recognition is granted, payment collection, registry action or another enforcement measure may still be necessary. A realistic assessment separates the prospects of recognition from the practical prospects of obtaining the desired result.

Legal framework considered
  • Applicable bilateral, regional or multilateral treaty, if in force for the country and subject.
  • Argentine Code of Civil and Commercial Procedure, arts. 123 and 517–519, when that code governs.
  • Argentine Civil and Commercial Code, private international law provisions relevant to the particular family matter.
